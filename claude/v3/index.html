Why the best plan sits at a corner

The workshop problem from Lecture 2 (Rao, Example 3.2). A firm makes two machine parts. x1 = Part I made per week, x2 = Part II made per week (the slides call them x and y).

Why a corner?

Every plan on the red line earns the same profit k. Raising k slides the line, still straight and at the same slope, further out. The plans that are still possible get fewer as k grows. Because the edges of the shaded region are straight too, the last bit of the region the line touches is a corner. If the line happened to be parallel to an edge, it would be a whole edge, and that edge still ends in corners. So to find the best plan you only need to check the corners.

The corners of the region

Try this

  1. Drag the slider slowly to the right. Watch the bold part of the red line shrink until it is a single point. That point is a corner.
  2. Move your finger or mouse along the edge of the shaded area. The profit goes up as you approach the starred corner and goes down after you pass it.
  3. Point just outside the shaded area. The box tells you which machine has run out of time. Then find the grinding line: does it ever touch the shaded area?
